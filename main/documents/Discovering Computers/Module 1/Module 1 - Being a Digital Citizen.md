## Objective 1 - Digital Literacy

### Definition
- **Digital literacy** (also called computer literacy): having current knowledge and understanding of computers, mobile devices, the web, and related technologies
- **Digital citizen**: a person familiar with how to use technology to become an educated and productive member of the digital world

### Key Terms
- **Computer**: electronic device operating under control of instructions stored in its own memory; can accept data, process it to produce information, and store it
- **Memory**: electronic components storing instructions, data, and results
- **Internet**: global collection of millions of computers linked to share information
- **Network**: collection of two or more computers connected to share resources
- **Wi-Fi**: wireless data network technology providing high-speed connections without physical connection
- **Server**: powerful, high-capacity computer accessed via Internet/network; stores and serves files; grouped in data centers
- **Online social network**: online community for sharing interests, ideas, stories, photos, music, videos
- **Big Data**: large and complex data sources that defy easy management with traditional data processing methods

### Digital Distraction
- Using and relying on technology so much you don't pay attention to normal activities
- Examples: using smartphone during dinner, checking social media during class
- Can lead to lower grades, strained relationships, danger of injury/accident

## Objective 2 - Society's Reliance on Technology

### How Technology Impacts Society
- Revolutionized lives over the last quarter century
- Can more quickly/effectively access, search, share information
- Manage finances, calendar, tasks
- Play games, watch videos for entertainment
- Essential for acquiring jobs, global communications, international participation

### History of Computers
1. **Vacuum tubes** (1st generation): cylindrical glass tubes controlling electron flow; ENIAC, UNIVAC
2. **Transistors**: smaller, cheaper, more reliable; included tape/disk storage, memory, operating systems
3. **Integrated circuits** (1960s): thousands of vacuum tubes/transistors in silicon chip
4. **Microprocessor** (1971): Ted Hoff at Intel/IBM; "brains" of computer with CPU
5. **Personal computers**: Apple II (1978), IBM PC (1981); billions now in use
6. **Today**: connected devices sharing data via Internet/wireless; smaller, faster, more capable

### Internet of Things (IoT)
- Environment where processors are embedded in everyday products
- Things communicate with each other via Internet or wireless networks
- Includes alarm clocks, coffeemakers, thermostats, streetlights, navigation systems
- **Smart devices**: IoT-enabled devices capable of communicating, locating, predicting

### Embedded Computers
- Computer functioning as one component in a larger product with specific purpose
- Small, limited hardware but enhance capabilities of everyday devices
- Examples: printer ink monitors, vehicle cameras, blind spot warnings, backup cameras

### IoT Applications
- **ATMs**: withdraw cash, deposit checks; chip-and-pin technology
- **Kiosks**: freestanding booths for self-service (hotels, airports, healthcare check-in)
- **At Home**: remote device management, grocery lists, webcam monitoring, fitness trackers
- **In Business**: manufacturing sensors, retail inventory tracking, shipping monitoring

### The Digital Divide
- Gap between those who have access to technology/resources and those who don't
- Factors: age, income, location, education
- Affects learning, knowledge, opportunities
- Corporations, nonprofits, educational institutions, governments working to narrow it

## Objective 3 - Protecting Personal Information

### Privacy
- State/condition of being free from public attention to the degree you determine
- Right to be left alone to the level you choose

### Data Collection
- Data collected on almost all actions/transactions
- Sources: web surfing, purchases, surveys, smartphones, surveillance cameras
- **Data brokers**: hold thousands of pieces of info on millions of consumers; sell to third parties

### Identity Theft
- Using someone's personal information (name, SSN, credit card number) to commit financial fraud
- Can obtain credit cards, set up cellular accounts, rent apartments
- Victim charged for purchases; suffers damaged credit history

### How Personal Information Is Stolen
| Technique | Explanation |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Dumpster diving** | Retrieving discarded credit card statements, receipts, bank statements |
| **Phishing** | Deceiving you into revealing info via email or fake website |
| **Change of address** | Diverting mail to attacker's P.O. box |
| **Pretexting** | Pretending to be from legitimate research firm |
| **Stealing** | Stolen wallets/purses contain personal info |
| **Data mining** | Guessing passwords via social media queries (pet name, home state, etc.) |

### Privacy Protection Safeguards
- Shred financial documents before discarding
- Don't carry SSN in wallet or write on checks
- Don't provide personal info over phone or email
- Keep personal info in secure location
- Be cautious about social networking posts and who can view them
- Keep only last 3 months of financial statements; shred older ones
- Consider before allowing websites/apps to collect data
- Avoid websites requesting excessive personal information
- Be alert to unusual account activity
- Follow up on unfamiliar purchases
- Review financial statements monthly

### Strong Password Tips
- Don't use personal info (name, pet name, birth date, schools)
- Use at least 8 characters with mix of upper/lowercase, numbers, punctuation
- Change password frequently
- Don't use same password for all sites
- Avoid common sequences (12345678) or backwards spelling
- Manage passwords using an app

## Objective 4 - Technology in the Professional World

### Workplace Technology
- Nearly every job requires technology interaction
- Workers use smartphones, Internet, cloud to work remotely
- **Telecommuting**: working from home
- **Intelligent workplace**: technology enables workers to connect, communicate, use productivity software, meet via web conferencing
- **BYOD** (Bring Your Own Device): employees use personal devices for business
- Online collaborative productivity software for sharing/editing documents

### Technology in K-12 Education
- Social networking tools for events, group projects, anti-bullying
- Online productivity software for collaboration
- Interactive white boards for projecting/manipulating data
- **Intelligent classroom**: technology facilitates learning and communication

### Technology in Higher Education
- **LMS** (Learning Management System): web-based training sites for progress, tests, messaging
- Online lectures, classes, degrees
- Ebooks with digital assets (videos)

### Technology in Health Care
- **mHealth** (mobile health): healthcare professionals using smartphones/tablets; patients using digital devices to monitor conditions
- Apps for tracking prescriptions, medication reminders
- Medical monitoring devices (electronic bracelets)
- Smart pills with sensors/cameras
- 3-D printers for skin, prosthetics, casts
- **Telemedicine**: telecommunications technology for remote diagnosis/treatment

### Technology in Manufacturing
- **CAM** (Computer-Aided Manufacturing): streamline production
- Robots for dangerous, detailed, or monotonous work
- **M2M** (Machine-to-Machine) communications: machines communicating to perform tasks

### Technology in Transportation
- Handheld computers for scanning packages
- GPS for navigation, traffic avoidance
- Self-driving trucks using robotics
- Automated vehicles for independent transportation

## Objective 5 - Using Technology to Find a Career

### Professional Online Presence
- Often first thing recruiters see
- Tips:
  - Don't use humorous/informal names
  - Include professional photo
  - Upload PDF resume
  - Include links to videos, publications, digital content
  - Proofread carefully
  - Enable privacy settings on personal social media

### LinkedIn and Professional Networks
- Career-based social networking
- Search for jobs, research companies, join groups
- Share career info, communicate with contacts
- Online training courses to keep skills current

### Technology Certifications
- Demonstrate knowledge in specific area to employers
- Most require passing an exam (multiple choice or skills-based)
- Areas: application software, data analytics, database, web design, hardware, networking, OS, programming, cybersecurity
- Demonstrate commitment; help land a job

## Objective 6 - Convergence

### Definition
- **Convergence**: increasing integration of technological capabilities on a growing number of previously unrelated devices
- Same email, social network, gaming apps accessible on laptop, tablet, smartphone
- Overlap in capability is increasing

### Computer Devices
| Device | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Laptop** | Thin, lightweight mobile computer; screen in lid, keyboard in base; up to 7 lbs |
| **Ultrathin laptop** | Less than 1 inch thick, ~3 lbs or less |
| **Desktop** | Stationary; screen separate from tower, or all-in-one |
| **Tablet** | Thin, lightweight, touch screen; larger than phone, smaller than laptop |
| **Smartphone** | Internet-capable phone with calendar, address book, games, apps |
| **Ebook reader** | Mobile device for reading digital media (books, magazines) |

### Media and Gaming Devices
| Device | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Digital camera** | Creates digital images; wireless communication; review/modify images |
| **Digital media player** | Plays audio/video files; streaming capability |
| **Wearable device** | Small, mobile computing device designed to be worn |
| **Activity tracker** | Monitors heart rate, pulse, steps, sleep patterns |
| **Smartwatch** | Keeps time + communicates with smartphone for calls, messages, web, apps |
| **Game console** | Hardware for video games; connects to TV/screen |
| **Handheld game device** | Small, portable; includes small screen |

## Objective 7 - Legal and Ethical Responsibilities

### Digital Citizenship
- Being digitally literate ≠ being responsible digital citizen
- Digital citizens know when it's appropriate to copy/paste, how to credit sources, ramifications of copyright violations
- Involves: adhering to laws, following etiquette, knowing rights, keeping info secure, avoiding unhealthy tech habits

### Netiquette
- Rules of Internet etiquette
- Code of acceptable behaviors while online
- Covers web, social media, messaging, chat rooms, discussions, file transfer/storage

### Digital Ethics
- Set of legal and moral guidelines governing use of technology
- All digital citizens responsible for educating themselves about obligations and rights

### Determining Technology Use
1. **Is it legal?** Are there laws prohibiting this purpose?
2. **Is it ethical?** Does society generally approve/disapprove? What about professional/academic code of ethics?
3. **Is it moral?** Does this feel right based on your personal code of morals?

### Legal Issues
- **Cyberbullying**: bullying via digital devices/platforms (social media, forums, messaging, email)
  - Missouri statute defines as "bullying through transmission of communication by electronic device"
  - Schools required to report; perpetrators can be convicted of felony
- **Distracted driving**: driving while focusing on electronic devices
  - "Hands-free" laws in states like Massachusetts
  - Prohibits texting and other activities while driving

### Acceptable Use Policies (AUP)
- Documents listing guidelines and repercussions for using Internet and digital resources
- Reduces organization liability; clarifies what is/isn't fireable offense
- **Code of conduct**: rules against harm, unauthorized access, intellectual property theft, software piracy

### Plagiarism
- Copying or using someone else's work and claiming it as your own
- Schools have specific rules; students may sign acknowledgment
- Services like Turnitin check for plagiarized passages

### Green Computing
- Reducing electricity consumed and environmental waste when using technology
- **ENERGY STAR program**: encourages energy-efficient devices
- Personal efforts: purchase ENERGY STAR products, shut down overnight, donate equipment, use paperless communication, recycle, telecommute
- Organization efforts: consolidate servers, buy high-efficiency equipment, use sleep modes, recycle properly

## Objective 8 - Assistive Technologies

### Accessibility
- Practice of removing barriers preventing individuals with disabilities from interacting with data, websites, or apps

### Visual Impairment
- Change screen settings (text size, color)
- Voice input/output (computer speaks aloud or converts speech to text)
- **Braille printer**: prints information in Braille
- **Screen reader**: uses audio output to describe screen contents
- **Alt text** (alternative text): descriptive text added to images; screen reader reads aloud

### Hearing Impairment
- Programs/apps display words or visual clues instead of sounds
- **Captioning software**: displays scrolling text for video dialogue
- Cameras interpret sign language gestures into text

### Mobility Issues
- On-screen keyboard, larger keys, hand-mounted pointer
- Alternatives to mouse: hand pad, foot pedal, facial motion receptor, pneumatic instrument
- Less sensitive input devices for involuntary movements

### Intellectual Disabilities
- **Speech recognition**: input data verbally
- **Graphic organizers**: create outline/structure of information
- **Audio books**: read information aloud

### Legal Requirements
- **ADA** (Americans with Disabilities Act): requires companies with 15+ employees to accommodate physically challenged workers
- **IDEA** (Individuals with Disabilities Education Act): requires public schools to purchase or fund adaptive technologies
