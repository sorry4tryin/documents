## 6.1 Rotational Kinetic Energy

**Write the formula for rotational kinetic energy.**
<details><summary>Reveal</summary>
K_rot = ½Iω²  (also = ½Lω)
</details>

**What is total KE for a rolling object?**
<details><summary>Reveal</summary>
K_tot = ½mv² + ½Iω² — translational plus rotational
</details>

**How do you convert linear speed at a pivot to angular speed?**
<details><summary>Reveal</summary>
v = ωL → ω = v/L, where L is the distance from the pivot
</details>

**What happens to K when a disk moves from rotating on its center to rotating off-center at the same ω?**
<details><summary>Reveal</summary>
K increases — I increases by md² (parallel axis theorem), so ½Iω² is larger
</details>

**Can rotational KE be greater than, equal to, or less than translational KE for a free object in space?**
<details><summary>Reveal</summary>
Any of the three — no constraint links them (answer D type)
</details>

## 6.2 Torque & Work

**Write the rotational work formula and work-KE theorem.**
<details><summary>Reveal</summary>
W = τΔθ  and  W = ΔK_rot = ½I(ω² − ω₀²)
</details>

**What does the area under a τ vs. θ graph equal?**
<details><summary>Reveal</summary>
Work done on the system
</details>

**If torque doubles for the same time, how does work change?**
<details><summary>Reveal</summary>
Work ratio is 4:1 — because Δθ = ½αt² ∝ τ, so W = τΔθ ∝ τ²
</details>

**For a wheel slowing due to a block: list the 5-step process to find angular displacement.**
<details><summary>Reveal</summary>

1. F_friction = μ_k F_N
2. τ = F_f · R
3. W = ΔK_rot = ½Iω₀²
4. Δθ = W/τ
5. Simplify/substitute
</details>

## 6.3 Angular Momentum & Impulse

**Give both formulas for angular momentum.**
<details><summary>Reveal</summary>
L = Iω (rigid body)  and  L = rmv sinθ (point object about a point)
</details>

**What is angular impulse, and what graph quantity equals it?**
<details><summary>Reveal</summary>
ΔL = τΔt = IΔω; it equals the area under a τ vs. t graph
</details>

**A disk slides at constant velocity. Is its angular momentum about a nearby point zero?**
<details><summary>Reveal</summary>
No — L = rmv sinθ is constant and nonzero as long as θ ≠ 0°
</details>

**L as a function of θ (puck approaching rod) — what shape is the graph?**
<details><summary>Reveal</summary>
Sine curve — L = rmv sinθ, peaks at θ = 90°, zero at 0° and 180°
</details>

**What does the slope of an L vs. t graph represent?**
<details><summary>Reveal</summary>
Net torque (τ = ΔL/Δt)
</details>

## 6.4 Conservation of Angular Momentum

**When is angular momentum of a system conserved?**
<details><summary>Reveal</summary>
When the net external torque on the system is zero
</details>

**Clay drops onto a spinning platform. What can you say about the angular impulse each exerts on the other?**
<details><summary>Reveal</summary>
Equal in magnitude, opposite in direction — Newton's 3rd law (rotational)
</details>

**A student pulls masses inward. What happens to ω and why?**
<details><summary>Reveal</summary>
ω increases — I decreases (mass closer to axis), L = Iω is constant, so ω must increase
</details>

**Does angular momentum change when a puck sticks to a rod that is free to rotate?**
<details><summary>Reveal</summary>
No — the torques are internal to the disk-rod system, so L is conserved
</details>

## 6.6 Orbiting Satellites

**List all quantities that are constant in a circular orbit.**
<details><summary>Reveal</summary>
v, KE, U_g, E (mechanical energy), L (angular momentum), r
</details>

**In an elliptical orbit, what two quantities are conserved? What changes?**
<details><summary>Reveal</summary>
E and L are conserved; KE and U_g change (r changes, so F_g does work)
</details>

**Derive orbital speed for a circular orbit.**
<details><summary>Reveal</summary>
mv²/r = GMm/r² → v² = GM/r → v = √(GM/r)
</details>

**What is escape velocity and does it depend on the satellite's mass?**
<details><summary>Reveal</summary>
v_esc = √(2GM/r); no — satellite mass cancels when K + U = 0
</details>

**For an elliptical orbit, if r increases, what happens to K and U_g?**
<details><summary>Reveal</summary>
U_g becomes less negative (increases); K must decrease by the same amount to keep E constant
</details>

**What is the torque on a satellite in any orbit, and why?**
<details><summary>Reveal</summary>
Zero — gravity always points through the center (lever arm = 0), so τ = rF sinθ = 0
</details>
