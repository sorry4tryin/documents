- to be smart, an individual must be practical
	- information without practicality is a delusion
	- the most known way of becoming smarter comes from reading books. suppose reading books is no different than scrolling on social media: if individual A is scrolling on social media, therefore not retaining the information given–or understanding it, then individual B reading a book is not getting smarter. this concludes, that reading books will not make an individual smarter. to be smart, means to have the ability to understand something. when an individual understands something, they have the ability to interpret a select topic based on their own perspective. a similar aspect of understanding shows when an individual can either perform an action based on that select topic or provide instructions on how that action can be performed. 
		- example: to perform information gained through physical media (a book), an individual can start by writing essays. writing essays gives an individual the freedom to convey their own thoughts onto paper. therefore, if an individual is unable to write a essay on a select topic, with clarity and legibility, then the individual does not understand that select topic.
		- interpretation: impractical understanding occurs when an individual mindlessly scrolls social media, watches video content, or reads books. practical understanding occurs when an individual writes essays, tutors others, or creates projects. a sensible approach would be to rely on both aspects, rather than one; practical produces more results, but your brain needs impractical for absorption.
